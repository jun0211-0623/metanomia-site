Russia Expands the Rollout Stage of the Digital Ruble: Individuals Get Free Top-Ups and Payments up to 300,000 Rubles a Month

As of September 1 local time, Russia expanded the rollout stage of the digital ruble to cover major banks and, among those banks' clients, retail businesses whose revenue in the previous year exceeded 120 million rubles. This is a phased expansion following the pilot that began in 2023, and it does not mean full commercial rollout has been completed. The digital ruble is not a private stablecoin or a public blockchain token but a new form of the national currency, added to cash and non-cash money, for which the Bank of Russia bears direct issuance responsibility. Individuals and legal entities may each open only one account on the central bank platform through a linked bank app, while sole proprietors may hold two accounts, one personal and one for business. Individuals can top up as much as 300,000 rubles a month from an existing bank account, whereas there is no such limit on corporate top-ups. Use is free of charge for individuals, and companies are also exempt from fees until the end of 2026. Participation is voluntary. If an error or dispute arises, the matter should first be raised with the linked bank, and if it is not resolved, the user can turn to the Bank of Russia.

Metanomia View

The digital ruble may improve payment convenience, but because the central bank platform manages accounts and transaction records, questions of privacy and data control follow. It must be disclosed transparently who can access transaction information, under what conditions, how long it is retained, and what appeal procedures apply in cases of error or freezing. Since use is voluntary, what matters is not the introduction of the system itself but how much the actual number of accounts, participating merchants, and payment volume grows. Going forward, the trend in usage should be watched alongside how the use of cash and bank deposits changes.
