Injective Affiliate's Registration as a US SEC Transfer Agent Takes Effect

Injective is a finance-focused Layer 1 blockchain that handles spot and derivatives trading through an on-chain order book, and it has recently been expanding into the issuance and management of tokenized securities and real-world assets (RWAs). The project's affiliate, Injective Institutional Services, announced on August 19 local time that its registration as a transfer agent with the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) had taken effect. A transfer agent is similar to Korea's registrar for share transfers: acting on a mandate from the issuer, it maintains the official register of securities owners and manages changes in ownership as well as the exercise of rights such as dividends and voting. This is not the first registration by a blockchain company. In March of this year, tokenized securities firm Securitize was designated as the first digital transfer agent to support blockchain-based securities issuance on the tokenized securities platform being prepared by the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE). A staff FAQ from the SEC's Division of Trading and Markets explains that a registered transfer agent may use a distributed ledger as all or part of the official shareholder register, provided it meets requirements for security, accuracy, currency, reporting, and preservation. Injective plans to manage holder and jurisdictional restrictions, separation of issuance and redemption authority, freezing of restricted addresses, and suspension of transfers through Injective Mint and its RWA module. That said, this registration does not mean the SEC has approved all of Injective's tokens or products, and staff of the SEC's Division of Corporation Finance, Division of Investment Management, and Division of Trading and Markets stated in a January 28 statement that the form of issuance and the method of recording holders do not change the application of the federal securities laws. The announcement did not disclose which specific securities the affiliate actually maintains official registers for, or the scale of what it processes.

Metanomia View

The core of tokenization is not simply creating tokens but connecting those records to actual legal rights. When a registered transfer agent operates an on-chain register, trading and changes in rights can be managed within the same record-keeping system. But the authority to correct erroneous records or freeze addresses remains with the transfer agent and the issuer. Korea's tokenized securities (STO) framework likewise needs to clarify the standards under which on-chain records are recognized as legal ownership, and which party bears responsibility for correcting record errors and for processing investors' exercise of rights such as dividends, voting, and redemption.
